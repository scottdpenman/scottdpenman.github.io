Envisioning the Future Aquarium

Year
2018
Role
Researcher, Workshop Leader
Context
MIT Design Lab — Sponsored by the New England Aquarium

In 2018 — the New England Aquarium's 50th anniversary year — the MIT Design Lab and the Media Lab partnered with the Aquarium to host a one-week workshop imagining what an aquarium experience might look like in the decades ahead. As researcher and workshop leader, I helped organize and facilitate a group of students from MIT and affiliated universities in generating, prototyping, and presenting future aquarium concepts.

The workshop produced a broad range of proposals: automatically curated visitor guide maps that adapt in real time to the aquarium's live animal populations; underwater travel vessels that allow visitors to experience the ocean from within; and empathy-generating devices that mimic the sensory perception of fish, translating lateral line pressure and electroreception into human-interpretable experience.

Following the workshop, the Design Lab worked to distill the generated ideas into a coherent set of underlying themes. Three emerged most clearly: the future aquarium will personalize each visitor's experience, maximizing relevance and social engagement; it will immerse visitors in new sensory contexts both inside the building and in the surrounding urban environment; and it will deepen the educational and empathetic impact of each encounter with a living creature.

The aquarium of the future is less a building than a platform — for personalized encounter, sensory translation, and ecological empathy.
