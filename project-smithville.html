Smithville Master Plan + Municipal Complex

Year
Fall 2011
Role
Designer
Context
Mississippi State University — Studio / Professor Frances Hsu

On April 27, 2011, an EF5 tornado struck Smithville, Mississippi — one of the most destructive tornadoes in the state's history. The storm devastated the small town's commercial and civic core. In the months that followed, the question of how Smithville should rebuild became the subject of this studio project: not simply how to replace what was lost, but how to reconsider what a small American town could be in the 21st century.

The master plan connects Smithville's three primary infrastructural assets — the waterway, the railroad, and the highway — into a single strip of social and commercial activity. Rather than rebuilding in place, the plan reorganizes the town around this connective tissue, creating a framework that can attract investment and remain viable even when any single resource is depleted.

The plan doesn't rebuild Smithville as it was — it asks what Smithville could become.

The design of the municipal complex — comprising a civic center, library, and fire station — followed the master plan work and was developed as a group project. Concrete walls provide the strength and civic solidity expected of public buildings in this context. A computationally-generated wooden screen wraps the facade, softening the mass and providing a more welcoming public presence. The screen pattern was derived from site data and optimized for solar shading.
